# The Human Species: Every Member of Our Family Tree

## Who we are
Humans belong to *Homo sapiens*, the only surviving member of the genus *Homo*. Our lineage split from that of chimpanzees and bonobos roughly 6–7 million years ago. Early hominins such as *Sahelanthropus* and *Australopithecus* ("Lucy," about 3.2 million years old) walked upright long before brains grew large. The first members of *Homo* appeared around 2.8 million years ago. Below is each major human species, in rough order of appearance.

## Homo habilis — "handy man"
- **Time range:** about 2.4–1.4 million years ago
- **Region:** East and southern Africa
- **Key fossils:** OH 7 (the type specimen, Olduvai Gorge, Tanzania), OH 24, KNM-ER 1813 (Koobi Fora, Kenya)
- Slightly larger brain than australopiths and long associated with early Oldowan stone tools.

## Homo erectus — the first traveller
- **Time range:** about 1.9 million to roughly 110,000 years ago
- **Region:** Africa, western Asia (Dmanisi, Georgia), China and Indonesia
- **Key fossils:** Turkana Boy (KNM-WT 15000, Kenya), Java Man (Trinil, Indonesia), Peking Man (Zhoukoudian, China), Dmanisi skulls
- Human-like body proportions, Acheulean hand axes, and the first hominin to spread out of Africa.

## Homo heidelbergensis
- **Time range:** about 700,000–200,000 years ago
- **Region:** Africa and Europe (possibly Asia)
- **Key fossils:** Mauer jaw (Germany, type specimen), Kabwe 1 / Broken Hill skull (Zambia), Boxgrove (England)
- Large-brained; often proposed as a possible common ancestor of Neanderthals and modern humans, though its exact place is debated.

## Homo neanderthalensis — Neanderthals
- **Time range:** about 400,000–40,000 years ago
- **Region:** Europe and western and central Asia
- **Key fossils:** Feldhofer Cave (Neander Valley, Germany), La Chapelle-aux-Saints (France), Shanidar (Iraq), Amud (Israel)
- Stocky, cold-adapted, skilled toolmakers who buried some of their dead. Most people outside Africa carry about 1–2% Neanderthal DNA.

## Denisovans
- **Time range:** at least about 200,000 to roughly 50,000 years ago (possibly later)
- **Region:** Asia — Siberia, the Tibetan Plateau, and likely Southeast Asia
- **Key fossils:** a finger bone and teeth from Denisova Cave (Russia), the Xiahe jaw (Tibet)
- Known mostly from DNA; they have no formal species name yet. Their genes survive in people today, especially in Melanesia, and help Tibetans live at high altitude.

## Homo floresiensis — the "hobbit"
- **Time range:** about 100,000–50,000 years ago (stone tools to about 50,000)
- **Region:** Flores, Indonesia
- **Key fossils:** LB1 from Liang Bua cave; older related fossils at Mata Menge (~700,000 years)
- About 1.1 m tall with a small brain, yet made stone tools.

## Homo luzonensis
- **Time range:** at least 50,000–67,000 years ago
- **Region:** Luzon, Philippines
- **Key fossils:** teeth, finger and toe bones and a femur from Callao Cave (named in 2019)
- Mixed traits: small, modern-looking teeth but curved finger and toe bones like much older hominins.

## Homo naledi
- **Time range:** about 335,000–236,000 years ago
- **Region:** South Africa
- **Key fossils:** more than 1,500 bones from the Dinaledi and Lesedi chambers of the Rising Star cave system
- Small brain but lived at the same time as early *Homo sapiens*; whether it deliberately placed its dead in the cave is still debated.

## Homo sapiens — us
- **Time range:** about 300,000 years ago to today
- **Region:** originated in Africa; now worldwide
- **Key fossils:** Jebel Irhoud (Morocco, ~300,000 years), Omo Kibish (Ethiopia, ~230,000+), Herto (Ethiopia, ~160,000)
- Groups left Africa in a major dispersal about 70,000–50,000 years ago, reaching Australia by at least 50,000 years ago, Europe about 45,000 years ago and the Americas by at least 15,000–20,000 years ago, interbreeding with Neanderthals and Denisovans along the way. Farming began about 12,000 years ago; today more than 8 billion people live on every continent.

## Quick comparison
| Species | When (years ago) | Where |
|---|---|---|
| H. habilis | 2.4M–1.4M | East & southern Africa |
| H. erectus | 1.9M–~110k | Africa, Asia |
| H. heidelbergensis | 700k–200k | Africa, Europe |
| Neanderthals | 400k–40k | Europe, W & C Asia |
| Denisovans | ≥200k–~50k | Asia |
| H. floresiensis | 100k–50k | Flores, Indonesia |
| H. luzonensis | ≥50k–67k | Luzon, Philippines |
| H. naledi | 335k–236k | South Africa |
| H. sapiens | 300k–today | Africa → worldwide |

## Takeaway
For most of the last few hundred thousand years, several kinds of humans shared the planet. We are the last one standing — and we still carry pieces of our cousins in our DNA.
